Pl. 00 — Statement Est. 1998  ·  The Americas & the Digital

The central question

What happens to cultural knowledge when institutions collapse, communities migrate, and algorithms increasingly shape what becomes visible?

An integrated research program at the intersection of the digital humanities, Latin American and Caribbean studies, and the politics of memory — building the infrastructure through which endangered cultural knowledge is preserved, described, and made public.

Pl. 01 — The Program

A research program, not a portfolio.

SCALE     Institutional infrastructure
METHOD    Archival · computational · public
REACH     Six partner nations

This work begins from a premise that is at once scholarly and urgent: that the archive is never neutral, and that in moments of crisis it becomes a site of contest. When institutions fail and populations move, the record of a culture does not simply disappear — it fragments, disperses, and reassembles in the hands of those who carry it.

The program treats the digital not as a solution but as a condition. Algorithms now mediate what a society can remember and what it is allowed to forget. To study cultural memory today is therefore to study code, metadata, and the classificatory logics that decide what enters the record at all.

Across five interlocking initiatives — an archive, a studio, a book, a graduate certificate, and a laboratory — the research builds durable infrastructure: not artifacts to be admired, but instruments others can use. The measure of the work is not what it displays, but what it makes possible.

“To preserve is to make a decision about the future. Every archive is an argument about who will be remembered.”

Fig. 03 — Chronology

An archival timeline

1998

First rescue

The founding gesture — a single collection of periodicals digitized before it could be lost. The seed of Archivo Venezuela.

2013

Ad-hoc rescue becomes method: description standards, cataloguing protocols, and interface design formalized into a studio practice.

2017

Collecting expands across three continents as communities in dispersal become collaborators, not subjects.

2020

The practice becomes a curriculum — training scholars to build and steward, not only to interpret.

2023

Pl. 07 — About

The scholar behind the program

Patricia Valladares-Ruiz works at the intersection of the digital humanities and Latin American and Caribbean studies, where questions of memory, migration, and technology converge. Her research asks how cultural knowledge survives the collapse of the institutions meant to protect it — and what role computation now plays in deciding what a society remembers.

Over two decades she has built an integrated program — archive, studio, book, curriculum, and laboratory — that treats research infrastructure as a scholarly act in its own right. Her work is conducted in partnership with communities in dispersal and with libraries, archives, and cultural institutions across the Americas and Europe.
