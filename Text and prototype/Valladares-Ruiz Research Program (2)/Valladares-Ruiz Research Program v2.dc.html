Fig. 03 — Critical AI

The same object, read two ways.

A machine classifies what it can measure. A community knows what it means. The gap between them is where memory is lost.

AV-2017-0555 [ photograph ]
Caracas, 2017
Machine readingconf. 0.71
crowd street daytime people · 40+ urban area event

Classified as “public gathering.” No date beyond EXIF. No names. No reason.

Community reading

A vigil, not a riot. The candles are cropped out. The banner names the disappeared.

Photographed by a participant, later exiled. The image left the country on a phone. What the model reads as “crowd,” a mother reads as her son.

→  What the machine cannot see is precisely what the archive exists to keep.

Pl. 04 — The scholar, through the work

She does not appear here. Her questions do.

What she asks

How does a culture remember itself once its institutions are gone?

How she works

Archival, computational, and public — with communities as collaborators, never as subjects.

Who she works with

Six partner institutions across the Americas and Europe; libraries, archives, and museums.

What remains

Infrastructure others can use — an archive, a studio, a book, a curriculum, a lab.

Patricia Valladares-Ruiz works at the intersection of the digital humanities and Latin American and Caribbean studies. For two decades she has treated the building of research infrastructure as a scholarly act in its own right.
